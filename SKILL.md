# Video Editing

A Resolve-first editorial workflow with optional specialist tools. This skill is instructions, not a Resolve plugin or a guarantee of autonomous control. Prefer the user's editor and existing project. Use only the tools needed for the requested result.

## Scope and current state
- Distinguish research, planning, source assembly, selective creative cutting, graphics, and delivery. Do not turn a question about capability into timeline edits.
- Inspect the live project, timeline, bins, tracks, frame rate, source paths and online state before editing. Historical track numbers are not authoritative. Preserve manual edits, bin organization, audio, grades and end cards outside the requested change.
- If the user requests every relevant take, retain full source ranges and alternate angles, avoid exact duplicates, and group with markers. Existing source in/out marks must not silently shorten the assembly. If asked for a finished cut, choose purposeful moments rather than force all sources in.
- Use supplied media only unless additional sourcing is authorized. Report offline or unreviewed sources and the actual inspection coverage.

## Inspect, decide, assemble
1. Probe source timing and media properties. Review representative frames, then inspect the action around proposed in/out points. A thumbnail does not establish the entire clip's contents or quality. Listen to dialogue and sound where relevant.
2. Define the audience, intended takeaway and duration from the request. Choose shots for action, focus, composition, continuity and emotional or product value. Every cut needs a reason.
3. Build an editable cut list with source identity, in/out, timeline position and purpose. Use a hook, progression and payoff when it fits; preserve customer meaning and actual preparation order. Do not invent endorsements or preparation steps.
4. Shape rhythm through varied holds, action cuts, sound bridges and deliberate pauses. Beat analysis is a guide, not a mandate to cut every beat. Preserve breaths and satisfying actions. Review the finished sequence at normal speed.
5. Finish only requested audio, colour, captions and graphics. Match the brief rather than apply a generic look. Keep original media recoverable and do not replace a manual assembly without authorization.

## Completion evidence
Verify timeline identity/settings, edited ranges, source timing, text coverage, rendered frames across transitions and animation extremes, and audio where applicable. Verify saving separately from rendering. State exactly what was checked and what remains unverified. A running tracker, queued export, successful command, or one attractive frame is not proof of a complete edit.

Report the useful result concisely, show a relevant preview when available, and identify real limitations without repeatedly asking the user to perform already-authorized steps. If controls repeatedly fail, stop issuing mutations, preserve the last confirmed save, and explain the concrete blocker. A skill cannot repair an inaccessible interface by assertion.
